Authenticity Starts with Traditional Indian Recipes

When people say “authentic Indian food”, they often think it means “very spicy”. But that is not correct.

Authentic Indian food means:

  • Recipes made the traditional way
  • Proper cooking time
  • Correct spice combinations
  • Taste that feels like real Indian home-style or restaurant-style food

Most traditional Indian recipes are not invented in one day. They are passed down, improved slowly, and cooked with experience. This is very common in North Indian cuisine, where flavour comes from:

  • Slow cooking
  • Layered spices
  • Balanced aromatics like ginger, garlic, cumin, coriander, cloves

Signs of Authentic Indian Takeaway

Here are the clear signs you should look for when ordering authentic indian takeaway:

  • Gravies feel fresh (not like reheated base sauce)
  • Whole spices are used (not only ready-made pastes)
  • The dish tastes regional (not just a “generic curry” flavour)

The Importance of Spice Balance (Not Just Heat)

A lot of people think Indian food is only about “heat” (spicy level). But in reality, authentic Indian food is about spice balance. A well-cooked Indian dish should feel:

  • Aromatic
  • Deep in flavour
  • Smooth in taste
  • Not burning your mouth

Here is what balanced spice looks like:

  • Proper spice ratios improve flavour without overpowering
  • Even mild dishes can have strong aroma
  • Spice level should support the dish, not control it

North Indian cuisine is known for rounded, creamy, and balanced flavour, especially in gravies and curries.

So next time you order Indian food takeaway, do not judge it only by “spicy or not spicy”. Judge it by how balanced it feels.

Freshly Cooked Curries vs Batch Cooking

This is one of the biggest differences between an average takeaway and a high-quality one.

Freshly cooked curries:

  • Keep their aroma
  • Taste cleaner
  • Travel better for takeaway

But batch-cooked curries often:

  • Lose flavour depth
  • Feel oily or heavy
  • Become watery after delivery
  • Taste the same across different dishes

This is important because when people order Indian food takeaway in London, they want restaurant-style taste at home – not a “reheated curry” feeling.

FAQs

1) Why does some Indian takeaway have too much oil?

This is normally the case when some Indian takeaways add more oil or ghee to make the curry look rich and shiny. Some Indian takeaways also add oil to hide the quality of the ingredients or to make the food last longer. However, a good Indian takeaway should not be greasy.

2) Does spicy food mean authentic Indian food?

Not at all. Many people believe that authentic Indian food is “very spicy,” but authentic Indian food is more about the balance of spices. In India, some foods are spicy, some are not, and some are sweet and creamy.

3) Why do some Indian curries taste the same in different takeaways?

When many curries taste almost the same, it means that the takeaway is using the same “base gravy” or sauce for most of the dishes. They may just add chicken, paneer, or vegetables, but the main flavor will be the same. This is normally the case when takeaways want to save time in the kitchen.

4) What is the easiest Indian dish to order if I’m trying Indian food for the first time?

If you are new to Indian food, it is always best to start with dishes that are flavorful but not too spicy. Good options include butter chicken, chicken tikka, dal, and paneer butter masala. These dishes are typically creamy, mild, and easy to handle. You can also have them with naan or rice.

5) What Indian food travels best for takeaway and delivery?

Not all Indian foods travel well, so this is a very valid question. The best Indian foods to take away are the ones that remain flavorful even after 20 – 40 minutes. Tandoori dishes such as chicken tikka or kebabs are excellent because they are dry and flavorful. Thick gravies such as butter chicken, paneer curry, or dal are also excellent because they do not become watery easily. Avoid dishes that are based on crispiness because they tend to become soft in takeaway boxes.

6) Why does takeaway naan sometimes become soggy or chewy?

This is primarily due to the presence of steam. When naan is packed hot inside a closed box or bag, the steam gets trapped inside and makes the naan soft. Also, if naan is kept above the curries or near the hot containers, it absorbs moisture quickly. Good takeaway restaurants pack naan properly and deliver it fresh. If you want the naan to stay better, it’s also helpful to eat it quickly after delivery.

7) What makes Indian tandoori food special compared to normal grilled food?

Tandoori food is cooked inside a tandoor, which is a traditional clay oven used in Indian cooking. It cooks food at high heat, which gives a smoky aroma and a juicy texture. The special thing is the marination – tandoori items are normally marinated in yogurt and spices, which makes the flavor go deep inside.

8) Are vegetarian Indian takeaway dishes actually filling enough?

Yes, Indian vegetarian food is usually very filling because it uses protein-rich ingredients like lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, and paneer. Unlike some cuisines where vegetarian food feels light, Indian vegetarian dishes are cooked with spices, gravy, and proper ingredients that make them satisfying.

9) How do I know if the Indian takeaway is freshly cooked?

Freshly cooked Indian takeaway will smell strongly and fresh the moment you open the box. The curry will also look thick and fresh, not watery or too oily. Also, you can taste the freshness of the spices. Fresh food tastes better and doesn’t have a flat taste. If it looks like the food has been reheated, you might notice dryness in the meat, lack of flavor, or a stale smell.

10) Is Indian takeaway a good choice for group ordering (with friends / office colleagues)?

Yes, Indian takeaway is one of the best choices for group ordering because it has variety. You can order 2 – 3 curries (combination of chicken/veg), one rice, a few naans, and some starters like tandoori dishes. Everyone can share and pick what they want. It is also easy to mix and match according to preferences – some people like mild food, some like spicy food, and some like veg. That’s why Indian takeaway is a great choice for office lunches and dinner meetings.
