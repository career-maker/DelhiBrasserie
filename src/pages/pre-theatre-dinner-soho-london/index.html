What Counts as a Proper Pre Theatre Dinner in Soho, London

A pre theatre dinner is a meal timed to finish before a show’s curtain-up time, usually 7.30pm in the West End. The idea is simple: sit down early enough, eat at a steady pace, and still make it to your seat with time to spare.

In Soho, this works well because:

  • Most West End theatres are a 5–15 minute walk away.
  • Restaurants know theatre timings and can pace your courses.
  • Soho itself has restaurants covering nearly every cuisine, from Indian to Italian to French.

Demand for this kind of dining has grown alongside theatre attendance. West End venues welcomed a record 17.64 million theatregoers in 2025, according to the Society of London Theatre and UK Theatre (SOLT/UK Theatre, 2026 report). More seats filled means more diners looking for a reliable pre theatre meal nearby.

Why Soho Is the Best Base for Dinner and a Show in London

Soho borders Shaftesbury Avenue, Leicester Square, and Piccadilly Circus, three of the busiest theatre clusters in the West End. That makes it one of the most practical areas to plan dinner and a show in London, and it’s a big reason Soho keeps showing up on lists of the best pre theatre restaurants London has to offer.

From Soho, you can typically reach:

  • Shaftesbury Avenue theatres in under 10 minutes on foot.
  • Leicester Square venues in 5–10 minutes.
  • Covent Garden and the Theatre Royal Drury Lane area in 10–15 minutes.
  • Piccadilly Circus and Trafalgar Square in a similar walking window.

This is why so many searches for restaurants near West End theatres point straight back to Soho. It isn’t just central; it’s genuinely walkable, and pre theatre dining soho has stayed a strong search topic for exactly that reason.

How to Plan the Perfect Evening Before a West End Show

Best Time to Eat Before a West End Show

Most West End performances start at 7.30pm on weekdays, with earlier starts (around 7pm) on some Saturdays and matinees at 2.30pm or 3pm. Sitting down for dinner between 5.30pm and 6.15pm gives most diners enough time for a full meal without watching the clock.

What to Order for a Relaxed Pre Theatre Meal

If you’re comparing the best pre theatre menus London has to offer, look for a fixed set menu with a starter, main, and dessert. These are built for a steady pace, not a rushed one. At Delhi Brasserie, a typical pre-theatre dinner in London usually includes the following dishes:

  • A shared starter such as tandoori-cooked meats or vegetable pakoras
  • A main course paired with rice or freshly baked naan
  • A lighter dessert, or simply coffee if time is tight

Guests with more time before their show, or those without a strict curtain deadline, can also try the set menus, which include a fish or meat main, bread, vegetables, rice, and a dessert.
