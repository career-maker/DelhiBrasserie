There’s plenty to do around Leicester Square before or after dinner. You can take a short walk over to Piccadilly Circus, catch a West End show, stop by the Leicester Square statues for a few photos, or wander through the markets in Covent Garden.

If you’d rather keep things relaxed, there are also plenty of nearby bars where you can sit back and enjoy the evening. Most of these spots are within a 10-minute walk of Soho, so it’s easy to plan a fun evening around dinner at a restaurant like Delhi Brasserie on Frith Street.

This guide covers the best things to do near Leicester Square, grouped by what fits before dinner, what suits an after-dinner stroll, and what works well if you’re heading to a show. If you’re still deciding what to do in Leicester Square tonight, the short version is: pick one attraction, one walk, and one place to eat, and build the evening around your dinner time

Why Leicester Square Is a Good Base for an Evening Out

Leicester Square sits in the middle of London’s West End, right on the edge of Soho. It’s surrounded by cinemas, theatres, and restaurants, which is why so many people searching for things to do in Leicester Square London end up planning dinner around it too.

A few reasons it works well for an evening out:

  • Leicester Square Underground station is served by both the Northern and Piccadilly lines.
  • Leicester Square Underground station connects to the Northern and Piccadilly lines.
  • Several major cinemas, including Odeon Luxe Leicester Square, sit directly on the square.
  • The TKTS booth sells same-day and advance West End theatre tickets at the square’s south side.

Soho restaurants close by, including Delhi Brasserie at 44 Frith Street, mean you don’t need to travel far between your activity and your table.

Things to Do Near Leicester Square Before Dinner

If you’re arriving early for a dinner reservation, the square itself gives you plenty to see in under an hour.

Photo Spots and Statues

The statues are among the easiest Leicester Square photo spots to fit into a short walk, and one of the most photographed Leicester Square attractions overall. Popular photo stops include:

  • The Charlie Chaplin statue
  • The Mr. Bean statue
  • The Gene Kelly statue
  • The Mary Poppins statue
  • The Harry Potter statue

These sit close together, so you can see all of them in about 15 to 20 minutes.

The Swiss Glockenspiel Clock

Near the square’s entrance, a large steel clock called the Swiss Glockenspiel chimes on the hour. Figures inside the clock move to the sound of bells, and it was originally a gift from Switzerland and Liechtenstein. It’s a quick, free stop if you time your walk around the hour.

Family Attractions

Families looking for family attractions near Leicester Square have several indoor options within a few minutes’ walk:

  • M&M’s World, a multi-floor candy store
  • The LEGO Store
  • Ripley’s Believe It or Not! museum

These work well for filling time before a dinner reservation, especially with children in the group.

Places to Visit Near Leicester Square for Culture and History

For visitors who want more than photo spots, a short walk opens up some of the more iconic places near Leicester Square, and some of the better-known London West End attractions overall.

National Portrait Gallery and Trafalgar Square

Trafalgar Square is roughly a 10-minute walk from Leicester Square. The National Portrait Gallery sits just off the square and holds portraits of notable British historical figures. Trafalgar Square itself is a good stop for its fountains, Nelson’s Column, and the surrounding architecture.

Covent Garden

The Covent Garden to Leicester Square walk takes about 8 to 10 minutes on foot. Covent Garden has a covered market, street performers, boutique shops, and a mix of casual and formal dining. It’s a good pre-dinner stop if you enjoy browsing shops or want to catch a street performance.

Piccadilly Circus

Walking from Piccadilly Circus to Leicester Square takes around 5 to 7 minutes. Piccadilly Circus is known for its illuminated advertising screens and its location as a junction of several major shopping streets, including Regent Street.

Leicester Square Entertainment for Theatre-Goers

Leicester Square sits at the heart of London’s Theatreland, which makes it a natural stop for pre-theatre or post-theatre plans.

Booking Theatre Tickets

The TKTS booth on the square sells tickets for West End shows, often at a discount compared to box office prices. It’s a useful stop if you haven’t booked a show yet and want to line up a performance around your dinner time.

Pre-Theatre Dining

Many restaurants near the square, including Delhi Brasserie, offer earlier seating and shorter menus designed around show times. Booking a table an hour or two before curtain call gives you time to eat without rushing.

Cinemas on the Square

Leicester Square is home to some of London’s largest cinemas, including Odeon Luxe Leicester Square, which regularly hosts red-carpet film premieres. Vue and Cineworld also operate cinemas directly on the square, making it a common stop for a film before or after dinner.

Leicester Square Nightlife for After Dinner

Once dinner wraps up, the area around Leicester Square keeps going well into the evening.

Leicester Square nightlife options include:

  • Bars within a short walk of the square, ranging from cocktail lounges to late-night pubs
  • Some of the best nightclubs in Leicester Square and the surrounding streets, popular with both locals and visitors
  • Late-night cinema screenings for those who prefer a quieter finish to the evening
  • Ronnie Scott’s Jazz Club on Frith Street, two doors from Delhi Brasserie, known for live jazz performances

For a lower-key finish, a walk through Soho’s side streets after dinner takes you past bars, cafes, and independent shops that stay open into the night.

Date Ideas Near Leicester Square

If you’re after date ideas Leicester Square offers for two people rather than a group, a few combinations stand out:

  • Dinner followed by a West End show
  • An early film at Odeon Luxe Leicester Square, then dinner
  • A walk through Covent Garden before a later dinner reservation
  • Dinner followed by live jazz at Ronnie Scott’s, a short walk from Frith Street

Booking dinner first and building the rest of the evening around it usually works better than the other way around, since restaurant slots near the square fill up quickly on weekends.
