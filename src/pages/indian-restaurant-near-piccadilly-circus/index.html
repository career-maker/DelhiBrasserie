Why This Part of London Is Built for Food

Piccadilly Circus sits at the meeting point of five major streets, and it borders three of London’s most famous food destinations: Soho, Chinatown, and Theatreland. Few areas in the city pack this much dining variety into such a small radius.

A few facts set the scene:

  • Piccadilly Circus Underground station recorded 28.49 million entries and exits in 2024, making it one of the busiest transport hubs in Central London (Transport for London, 2024).
  • London’s West End welcomed over 17.1 million theatregoers in 2024, an 11% rise on pre-pandemic levels, according to the Society of London Theatre (SOLT, 2025).
  • Chinatown London, centred on Gerrard Street, currently has around 78 restaurants, 53 shops, and 12 bars packed into a few streets (Chinatown London community data).

That combination of theatre crowds, tourist footfall, and dense restaurant clusters is exactly why restaurants near Piccadilly Circus cover such a wide range of cuisines, from Cantonese roast duck to North Indian tandoori. Tables get booked up fast around show times, so it’s worth reserving ahead rather than turning up on the night.

Indian Restaurant Near Piccadilly Circus: Delhi Brasserie

For diners who want authentic North Indian cooking rather than a generic curry house, Delhi Brasserie on Frith Street is one of the most established options in the area. It has been serving Soho since May 1985, making it one of the longest-running Indian restaurants in Central London.

Key details:

  • Location: 44 Frith Street, Soho, London W1D 4SB — a short walk from Piccadilly Circus, Leicester Square, and Chinatown.
  • Hours: Open daily from midday to midnight, which suits both early dinners and post-theatre bookings.
  • Style: Traditional North Indian dishes — tandoori grills, biryanis, curries, and thalis — served in a warm dining room with high ceilings, white table linens, and tall windows over Frith Street.
  • Private dining: A self-contained basement room with its own bar, suited to office parties, birthdays, and group bookings.
  • History: Ronnie Scott, whose jazz club sits two doors away, was guest of honour when Delhi Brasserie opened. The restaurant has also hosted guests including Twiggy and Lenny Henry over the decades.

This mix of authentic cooking, flexible hours, and proximity to the theatres is why Delhi Brasserie regularly comes up when people search for the best restaurants near Piccadilly Circus for a proper Indian meal rather than a fast-casual option.

Best Restaurants in Chinatown London

Chinatown London sits just off Shaftesbury Avenue, a five-minute walk from Piccadilly Circus. It’s the most established Chinese food district in the UK.

A short history for context: the current Chinatown began forming on Gerrard Street in the 1950s, after Chinese immigrants moved from the East End’s Limehouse district. By the 1970s, the number of restaurants and businesses had expanded significantly, creating the neighbourhood that exists today.

What to expect from restaurants in Chinatown London:

  • Cantonese roast meats — duck, char siu pork, and crispy pork belly hanging in shop windows
  • Dim sum houses serving small plates through lunch and early evening
  • Hot pot restaurants for group dining
  • Bakeries and dessert shops for bubble tea, egg tarts, and pastries
  • Supermarkets stocking ingredients not found in typical UK grocery stores

Most China Town London restaurants sit within a three-street radius, so you rarely need to walk far to compare menus before choosing.

Gerrard Street, Lisle Street, and Wardour Street form the core of the district, marked by ceremonial gates and red lanterns. If you’re looking for the best places to eat in Chinatown London, this three-street stretch is where most of the well-known kitchens sit.

Chinatown vs. Piccadilly Circus: What’s the Difference?

Restaurants near Chinatown London and restaurants Piccadilly Circus are often treated as the same area, and in practice they nearly overlap. Chinatown sits just northwest of the Circus, past the top of Coventry Street.

For a slower, sit-down meal with table service — particularly before or after a show — Frith Street and the surrounding Soho streets, home to Delhi Brasserie, tend to suit theatre timings better than Chinatown’s busier, faster-turnover restaurants.

Best Restaurants Near Piccadilly Circus for Pre-Theatre Dining

Theatre timings matter more than most guides admit. A 7:30pm curtain usually means finishing dinner by 7:00pm at the latest, which rules out restaurants with long waits or slow service.

What to look for in a pre-theatre restaurant:

  • Fixed opening from midday so you’re not rushing a late lunch reservation
  • A table booking system that guarantees your seat before the show
  • A menu that doesn’t require a long wait, such as tandoori grills or set thalis
  • A short walk to the theatre, ideally under 10 minutes

Delhi Brasserie fits this pattern well. Its Frith Street address puts it within easy reach of the Piccadilly, Prince Edward, Gielgud, and Lyric theatres, and its daily midday-to-midnight hours cover both early pre-theatre dinners and later post-show meals without a kitchen cut-off in between.

Post-Theatre Dining Near Piccadilly Circus

After an evening show, many kitchens in Central London have already closed. This is where restaurants that stay open until midnight — or later on weekends — become genuinely useful rather than a nice-to-have.

Post-theatre diners generally want:

  • A kitchen still taking orders after 10:30pm
  • A relaxed atmosphere rather than a rushed last seating
  • Comfort food — curry, roast meats, or noodles — after a long evening

Delhi Brasserie’s midday-to-midnight schedule, seven days a week, is built around exactly this kind of demand from Soho’s theatre crowd.
